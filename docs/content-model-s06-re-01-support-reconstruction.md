### Literal boundary is invariant under legal endpoint retirement

For a current complex type t, follow its finite immediate-base path while it remains complex.
If that path first reaches anyType through a proper complex A with base anyType, call A its literal complex boundary.
If it instead first exits into a simple/generated scalar type, record the scalar-exit case rather than inventing a complex A.
The exact first simple exit need not be invariant: a later lawful cut can bypass S to a higher simple ancestor such as anySimpleType.

Suppose t has a genuine nonprimitive own-RE01 receipt with designated ancestor A.
Its legal endpoint becomes restriction of a primitive intermediate E with exact immediate base A.
The old designated A is already on t's old ancestry, has immediate base anyType and cannot itself have an NP retirement.
When the designated A is complex, the new path t -> E -> A retains the exact same complex boundary A.
With a simple designated ancestor, the middle E has that exact simple base and the path remains in the scalar-exit case, although its first simple exit can change.
It therefore does not enter a new complex ur-boundary family.

No genuine NP target can have designated ancestor builtin anyType itself.
If its immediate base were anyType, the construction receipt would be primitive.
Otherwise the finite ancestry has a proper first type with immediate anyType base, and that proper type is the selected ancestor.
On a simple-content path that proper type may be builtin anySimpleType or another source-qualified scalar boundary; it is not a new complex A.

Consequently a fresh complex A with exact base anyType can never be the middle of a genuine original/P NP endpoint.
A restriction A is not a middle at all; an extension A would require designated ancestor anyType, which the preceding NP argument excludes.
It may be a designated ancestor or middle for a fresh target only according to the actual exact geometry, and those events stay inside its family.
